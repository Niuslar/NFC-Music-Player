NFC Player
Case Printing

Eight printed parts in two finishes: wood-fill PLA for the sides, front and lid, and charcoal PETG for the back, the keys and the card guide. Which filament each part takes, how it lies on the bed, the settings, the wood grain and how to finish it.

The largest part, the chassis, is 200 × 128 mm and fits a 220 × 220 mm bed. Every file is in millimetres; print at 100%.

Parts and finishes

Print one of each. The parts you see from outside are wood-fill: PLA filled with wood fibre, sanded and stained after printing. The parts that have to bend or take wear are PETG, which is tougher. The parts nobody sees can be any colour of PETG you have.

Fit gauge

A small plate, 90 × 36 × 4.2 mm, with one of every fit that matters. Print it flat, brass pocket up, in wood-fill PLA on exactly the settings you will use for the case, and try your parts in it before printing anything large. If you print the card guide in PETG, a second gauge in PETG checks its card slot too; it is a small print.

Wood grain (optional)

The plain chassis.stl and front.stl already look like wood once they are printed in wood-fill and stained. For a grain you can see and feel, wood.py carves one into them from a picture of wood you choose:

  1. Save a picture of wood grain as wood-grain.png in this folder, with the grain running across it from side to side. Dark parts are cut deepest. Neither the picture nor the grained STLs are in the repository: they are large, and they come from your own picture.
  2. Run blender -b -P build.py, then blender -b -P wood.py, from this folder (see Rebuilding for what you need). It writes STL/chassis-wood.stl and STL/front-wood.stl.
  3. Print those instead of the plain ones, the same way up and on the same settings.

The grain is cut at most 0.4 mm deep, and only inwards, so every face where parts meet keeps its size. It goes into the chassis's two sides and bottom chamfers and the front panel's edges, and fades out near the floor and the top, so the first layer stays clean and the lid sits flush. Nothing is cut into a face that prints on the bed or over support. By default the grain runs across the top and down the sides, like a waterfall edge; set WOOD_GRAIN=front-to-back to run it along the sides instead, or WOOD_IMAGE to try another picture without replacing yours.

The grain is drawn by the outer wall. Turn off fuzzy skin, and any slicer setting that drops detail smaller than the nozzle. Slow outer walls help most.

Finishing

Every part

  • Pick off any strings, and check every hole and slot against what the fit gauge showed.
  • Don't drill the screw pilots out: the M3 and M2 screws cut their own thread in them. Drive each screw in once by hand before assembly, so it goes in easily later.

Wood-fill parts: chassis, lid and front

  1. Sand lightly by hand, along the grain, with 240 then 400 grit, just enough to take the fuzz off the layer lines. Go gently on the grained parts: the grain is only 0.4 mm deep.
  2. Wipe on a wood stain (the render shows walnut) and wipe it off again after a minute or so. It soaks into the fibre and gathers in the carved grain and the layer lines, darkening them, which is what brings the grain out. A second coat makes it darker.
  3. Let it dry fully, then seal with wax, hard wax oil or a matt clear coat.
  4. Keep stain and finish out of the key holes, the encoder hole and the screw pilots. There is only 0.2 mm round each key cap, and a thick coat inside a key hole makes the key stick. Keep it thin in the brass pocket too, so the brass still sits flush.

Try the stain on the fit gauge first: wood-fill takes stain differently from brand to brand.

Key carrier

The symbols are cut 0.6 mm into the key faces. Fill them with paint if you like, a light colour on the charcoal, and wipe the faces clean before it dries. Keep paint out of the slots beside the bridges, or they will stick.

Rebuilding after a change

You need three free programs, on Windows, macOS or Linux: Blender (the scripts were made with Blender 5.2), KiCad 10 or later, and Python 3 with Pillow (pip install pillow). The commands below call Blender blender: put it on your PATH, or type the full path to the program instead. python is your own Python 3 (it may be python3 or py on your system), except for board.py, which needs the Python that comes with KiCad: on Windows, run it from the KiCad Command Prompt; on Linux, use the system python3 that KiCad installs into; on macOS, the python3 inside KiCad.app.

Edit the values at the top of case.py, then run these from the NFC-Player-Case folder in order. The first rewrites the STLs, the key carrier from keycaps.py among them, the OpenSCAD copy and the brass outline, and reports any part that collides with another. The OpenSCAD file is a generated copy and has not been run through OpenSCAD.

  1. blender -b -P build.py
  2. In the front-board folder, with KiCad's Python: python board.py
  3. blender -b -P wood.py (only for the wood-grain version)
  4. python drawings.py
  5. blender -b -P pictures.py
  6. blender -b NFC-Player-Render.blend -P render.py
  7. python guide.py

The second step redraws and reroutes the front board, runs KiCad's electrical and design rules checks, and rewrites its Gerber files. Run it whenever you move the keys, the screws or the board's outline. The last step writes this guide, the assembly guide and the front board's section of the electronics guide. The model checks that parts fit each other and the stand-ins. It cannot check print tolerances, your exact boards, heat, or how well the NFC reader works next to brass and stored cards: those need a bench test.
