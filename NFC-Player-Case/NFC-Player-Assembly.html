Published drawings did not settle these, so each is a named value at the top of case.py with a sensible default. Measure your parts, change the value and rebuild before printing the front or rear panel or cutting brass.

Print

Millimetres, 100% scale. Start with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, four walls and 20% infill in PETG. The largest part is 200 mm long and fits a 220 × 220 mm bed.

FileOrientation and notes
fit-gauge.stlFlat, pocket up. Print it first. It has a switch opening, the encoder hole and its pocket, 3.3, 2.55 and 2.9 mm holes, the 55.4 × 1.65 mm card slot and a corner of the brass pocket.
chassis.stlFloor down, no support. Clear the horizontal screw pilots in the towers by hand.
lid.stlTop surface down, with support under the two curved edges.
front.stlInside face down, brass pocket up, no support.
rear.stlFlat, either face down.
card-guide.stlFoot down, brim if needed. Keep support out of the card channel.
display-cradle.stlBase down, as it stands in the case. No support.

Hardware

  • M3 screws that thread straight into the print (try one in the gauge first, and drive by hand): four M3 × 10 for the front (they pass the brass and panel), four M3 × 8 for the rear, four low-profile M3 × 6 for the lid (head at most 6 mm across and 1.5 mm tall), two M3 × 6 for the card guide and two for the screen cradle.
  • Four M2.5 standoffs 5 mm tall, four M2.5 × 6 screws from below, and four more on top for the Pi.
  • Four Kailh Choc V1 switches with keycaps up to 17.5 mm wide. An M7 encoder with a 6 mm shaft, and a knob up to 34 mm.
  • A panel-mount USB-C socket on a short lead ending in micro-USB.
  • 0.8 mm brass sheet and thin double-sided tape. Four feet at least 5 mm tall. Foam pads, one small cable tie, and female jumper leads for the wiring.

Assembly

Printed part fitted in this stepPrinted parts already in placeBrassArrow: direction of fitting

1Fit the brass

  1. Deburr the brass and try it in the pocket. It should drop in with a hair of clearance and sit flush with the rim.
  2. Stick it down with thin double-sided tape, lining the screw holes up with the panel's.

2Fit the switches and the encoder

  1. Push the four switches in from the front, in the order previous, play/pause, next, back (left to right from the front), until they click.
  2. From behind, put the encoder's bushing through its hole so the body sits in the rectangular pocket.
  3. On the front: washer, nut, then the knob on the shaft.

3Fit the front panel and the screen

  1. Screw the front panel to the chassis with four M3 × 10 screws.
  2. Screw the cradle loosely to its two floor bosses with M3 × 6 screws.
  3. Slide the screen board down into the grooves of both uprights, glass towards the window and connector towards you.
  4. Look through the window and slide the cradle sideways until the lit area is centred, then tighten its screws. A foam pad between the board and the lid stops it lifting.

4Mount the Pi, splitter and DAC

  1. Screw four standoffs up through the two floor slots and leave them loose.
  2. Screw the Pi onto them, header at the front. Plug the splitter onto the Pi and the DAC onto the splitter's rear header, checking pin 1 each time.
  3. Slide the whole stack back until the DAC's sockets sit just inside the rear window, then tighten the standoffs from below.

5Fit the card guide and the NFC reader

  1. Screw the guide to the two floor bosses with M3 × 6 screws. The slot leans back toward the top.
  2. Set the reader's switches for SPI, then stand it on the shelf with its antenna side against the guide and its header at the top.
  3. Hold it with one cable tie round guide and reader. The tie passes outside the card slot.

6Wire it

Every wire runs to the splitter's spare header. Follow the pin tables in the build guide (docs/index.html, step 5); the top view under What goes where shows the routes.

  • Keep wires out from under the card tray, which comes down to 52 mm over the left two thirds of the case, and clear of the card slot.
  • Leave enough slack that the front panel can come off with the wires attached.

7Fit the rear panel

  1. Mount the USB-C socket in the rear panel and plug its lead into the Pi port marked USB (not PWR IN).
  2. Screw the rear panel on with four M3 × 8 screws.
  3. Check that an RCA plug and a headphone plug both seat fully through the window.

8Close up

  1. Before closing for good, test card reading with the brass fitted and five cards in the tray.
  2. Lower the lid between the panels and fit its four low-profile screws, by hand.
  3. Stick the feet on, clear of the floor slots and the vents.

Brass face

Cut it from 0.8 mm brass using Brass-Front-Template.svg. It is drawn at actual size, seen from the front: print it at 100% and check the scale bar measures exactly 100 mm before using it as a pattern. It has a screen window, one slot for all four switches (the keycaps cover it), the encoder hole and four screw holes, with centre marks for a punch.

The rectangular openings have square corners, which suits laser or waterjet cutting, or a fretsaw and file. Deburr, then try it in the printed pocket before sticking it down.

Rebuilding after a change

Edit the values at the top of case.py, then run these from this folder in order. The first rewrites the STLs, the OpenSCAD copy and the brass outline, and reports any part that collides with another. The OpenSCAD file is a generated copy and has not been run through OpenSCAD.

/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -P build.py
python3 drawings.py
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -P pictures.py
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b NFC-Player-Render.blend -P render.py
../.venv/bin/python guide.py

The model checks that parts fit each other and the stand-ins. It cannot check print tolerances, your exact boards, heat, or how well the NFC reader works next to brass and stored cards: those need a bench test.
